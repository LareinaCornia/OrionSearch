## Summarize the process of writing the paper and preparing the poster, including any surprises you encountered.

We initially used our poster as a design doc, writing out key points like details on our target dataset of `npm` docs, which was useful for clarifying initial requirements. We also left blank spaces earmarked for certain tasks, like performance evaluations and reflections, to have a kind of roadmap for the goals we were attempting to achieve.

Over the course of the project, as things came up, we modified and adjusted the poster to reflect that. For instance, when we confirmed we had time to implement the spell checker and debugging extensions, we allocated space for them at the expense of a more detailed and expansive architectural discussion and evaluation.

We also received feedback from our mentor regarding clarifying our target and why it is a challenging problem, and we were also encouraged to use visuals to explain concepts like our architecture rather than paragraphs or bullet points. In response to the recommendations, we created an architecture and pipeline diagram from scratch to replace our text, and we did some further research into why `npm` documentation is challenging to index and search.

We would not necessarily say we encountered any "surprises" during poster preparation, although we certainly encountered challenges. Posters are by definition quite limited in terms of space, so we were forced to decide on the most important points to include. We had to decide where we could simplify our architecture diagram vs. where we wanted to go into more depth, and which graphs best illustrated the advantages of distribution.
